## Close the day, now

This is the third time the client has asked for it to be simpler, and each time
the thing in the way was the same: the promoter was being asked to type what the
system already knew. It knows every sale they recorded, so it knows what sold
and what the shelf should hold. So on a normal night closing is now:

1. glance at today's sales — already there
2. type the cash — the card fills itself in, so the money always adds up
3. leave the counts alone unless something is actually different
4. press **Close the day**

The top-up is still on the same page, with anything low already filled in and
any other line — testers included — one pick away.

This also closes the item held from two rounds ago ("go back to the old
structure… easy and simple"): it is a single page again, and simpler than it
was then.

## Sign-in, and the one thing to raise with the client

"Like the PINs" means senior staff can **read** passwords again. That was the
PIN design's one compromise, and it is **worse for passwords** — people reuse
passwords on their personal e-mail and bank in a way they never reused a PIN.

What is in place: promoters never choose a password, so they cannot reuse one;
anybody who can choose their own is warned not to reuse one, in the dialog where
they do it; every look-up is recorded; and the accounts that can look passwords
up are the ones that also need the e-mailed code.

**The alternative** — seniors *set* a new password instead of *reading* the old
one — removes the readable copy entirely at no cost to how it feels to use.
Worth one sentence from Davy. Full write-up in [`auth.md`](./auth.md).
