### The exports

The CSV and PDF on a store's day and on Analytics are one detailed report,
for the dates and filter set. It opens as Zeoniq's does — **Product Sales
Listing**, then *Filters: Business Date from 1/10/2026 to 31/10/2026* — and
then:

1. **Sales by store**, where there is more than one.
2. **The product sales listing**, laid out like Zeoniq's (the PDF Davy sent
   on 18 November):
   - **Outlet:** *code - name*, then each **Item:** *code - name* under it,
     in the client's order, then every date it sold — none for the dates it
     did not — with **Business Date, Selling Price, Gross Amount, Discount,
     Net Sales, Item Qty, Trans. Count**.
   - A **Subtotal** under each item and under each outlet, and a **Grand
     Total** at the end.
   - What was given free is an item of its own, straight after what was
     sold, as Zeoniq keeps its F.O.C. items: *Wish 3 · 15ml (FOC)*.
   - Zeoniq's Tax, Charges, cost and gross-profit columns are left out
     ("no need to be exactly like this"; figures exclude SST). Nothing is
     discounted at the counter, so Gross Amount and Net Sales agree.
   - Trans. Count is how many sales a line was part of; a subtotal adds up
     its lines, as Zeoniq's does.
   - The CSV writes dates as 2026-11-02, which Excel reads as a date in any
     setting and shows as 2/11/2026; the PDF writes 2/11/2026.
   - The CSV lists every outlet in turn: the main stores, then the dealers,
     then consignment. The PDF draws one outlet as Zeoniq does; for several,
     it folds them into one listing, so a month of every store stays
     readable, and says the CSV has each outlet.
3. The dealers' and consignment partners' months keyed in, where nothing
   narrows by product or country.
4. **Payment**: cash, card (and e-wallet) as filed at each close.
5. **Closing stock**: deducted and on hand at each close. In the PDF only
   for a single day; a range's is in the CSV.
6. **Customers** by country, summed.
